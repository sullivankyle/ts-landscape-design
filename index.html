Services

From first sketch to the last plant.

Every project is planned around Santa Monica conditions: dry summers, salt air and the morning marine layer.

  • Garden design

    A complete plan for a front or back yard, covering layout, planting, materials and how the space looks through the seasons.

  • Water-wise planting

    California natives and Mediterranean plants chosen for coastal sun and wind, grouped by how much water they need.

  • Lawn replacement

    Turn thirsty turf into meadow, gravel or groundcover that still gives you somewhere to walk and sit.

  • Patios and hardscape

    Paving, steps, low walls, fire pits and seating areas that suit the house and the way you entertain.

  • Irrigation

    Drip layouts and smart controllers sized to the plants, so each one gets what it needs and nothing runs onto the sidewalk.

  • Outdoor lighting

    Low-voltage path, step and accent lighting laid out for evenings on the patio.

How a project runs

Five steps, in this order, with a clear decision for you at the end of each one.

  1. Step 1

    Site visit

    Tyler walks the yard with you, takes measurements and photos, and asks how you use the space.

  2. Step 2

    Concept plan

    A scaled drawing of the layout, paths and planting areas, with options to react to.

  3. Step 3

    Final design

    A plant list with botanical names, materials, irrigation and lighting, plus a written estimate.

  4. Step 4

    Installation

    Soil prep, hardscape, irrigation and planting, scheduled around your household.

  5. Step 5

    Aftercare

    A watering schedule and care notes for the first year, while the garden settles in.

About

Meet Tyler Sullivan.

TS Landscape Design is a new studio in Santa Monica. Tyler works with homeowners one project at a time, from the first walk around the yard to the last plant going in the ground.

Every plan starts on site. He measures the yard, notes where the sun and wind land, and asks how you want to use the space before anything gets drawn.

You get plans drawn to scale, a plant list with botanical names, and a materials list you can read without a landscape degree.
